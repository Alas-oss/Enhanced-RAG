Chapter 1

Once upon a time, in a small village nestled between two mountains, there lived a clockmaker named Elias who had not built a single clock in seven years.

The villagers no longer asked him why. They had grown used to the silence of his shop, the dust gathering on his tools, the sign above the door that still read "Elias & Sons, Fine Timepieces" though there had never been a son.

"You should open again," the baker's wife said one morning, pausing at his doorway with a loaf of bread in her arms. "The Hendricks girl is getting married. She'll want a clock for her new home."

Elias looked up from the window he'd been staring through for the better part of an hour. "I don't build clocks anymore," he said, the way he always said it, flat and final.

Chapter 2

That night, unable to sleep, Elias found himself standing in his workshop for the first time in years. Moonlight fell across the workbench, illuminating a half-finished mechanism he had abandoned the week his wife died.

He picked up a small gear, turning it over in his fingers. It still gleamed, untouched by rust, waiting.

"Just this once," he murmured to the empty room, and reached for his tools.
